# Handoff: week 3 stations

## Read this first

Class is today, Wednesday September 23, 12:00–2:45, Learning Lab studio. Week 3 is "Tools of the AV Trade." ## Decided

- **Four stations, twenty minutes each, groups rotate.** Roughly four groups of three. Marlon demos all four up front, then groups rotate. The audio station is cut: its lesson ("get close to the mic, which in practice means a wireless mic") is one sentence in the demo, and the wireless-mic-to-phone exercise moves into the phone station.
- **The four:** Studio lights · Studio cameras · Camera obscura · Phone. Marlon's read: two stations about the professional present (lights, cameras), two about the extremes (the deep past, the ubiquitous now).
- **Each station gets a printed card:** terms to master at the top, then a recipe for the activity. Cards are the student-facing artifact; the docs pages are the long version.
- **The recursion loop (camera pointed into its own live feed on the screen) lives at the camera obscura station**, not the pro-camera station. Reason: obscura is about passing an image through stages and what each stage adds.
- **Camera obscura is not the lens-comparison station.** There are only a few large-format lenses. Lens comparison happens at the studio-camera station, where many lenses fit the bodies.
- **Intake to the wall.** Two paths into `_media/`: phone shots go to the course Slack channel; studio-camera and lighting stations feed the ATEM, and Blue grabs stills in the control room. Both are manual today (Blue downloads into a folder). Assume **no metadata** on anything: ATEM stills have none, and iOS sharing often exports a downsized JPEG rather than the HEIC. Don't teach students to work around that today.
- **Weekly practice is non-AI this week.** Options Marlon named: a set of photos taken with attention to light; or use the phone-capture skills to level up your selfie video. Say explicitly that light panels and other gear can be borrowed.
- **Madeleine owns the camera obscura research and prints:** a couple of old drawings (Kircher 1646 cube, Zahn 1685 reflex box, Frisius 1545 are the obvious ones; the research briefing lists sources). Ruskin on the Gothic and Ingold's anti-hylomorphism are the texts to draw a line or two from.
- **Print:** glossary, tools, dates. The site should produce clean printable versions of those.

## The four stations

### 1. Studio lights

*Terms on the card (decided):* key light, fill light, backlight and its aliases (hair light: high and behind, top of the head; rim or edge light: the thin outline along the shoulder; kicker: from behind at an angle, raking the cheek), hard vs. soft light.
*Terms Claude suggests adding:* catchlight (the reflection in the eye that gives away the source's shape and position), separation, colour temperature (warm vs. cool, mixed sources).

*Recipe (decided in outline):*
1. Build three-point lighting one source at a time on a classmate. Key only. Add fill. Add backlight. Look at what each one does before adding the next.
2. Hard vs. soft with two shots of the same face: a small panel pulled far back, then a large panel brought in close. Look at the shadow edge and the nose-to-cheek transition. The lesson: apparent size of the source, not brightness.
3. Reverse-engineer a creator's setup. Marlon's version: stills of popular YouTubers and Instagram creators with readable setups, printed or on screen. Claude's suggestion, which Marlon said made sense but wanted vocabulary first: have students pull up a creator they follow on their own phone, freeze a frame, and read it (catchlights for source shape and position, shadow direction for the key, whether there's a rim), then rebuild it with the panels. Both can coexist: printed stills as the worked example, their own feed as the exercise.

*Note (Marlon):* the point is that they start developing a vocabulary for what they like and don't like.

### 2. Studio cameras

*Terms on the card (from Marlon's list; Claude's arrangement):* zoom vs. focus, aperture, depth of field, focal length, lens whacking.

*Recipe (decided in outline):*
1. Use the studio cameras with several lenses. Feel the difference between zooming and focusing. Change the aperture and watch depth of field change.
2. Misuse: lens whacking. Hold the lens off the body, tilt it, get the light leaks and the tilted plane of focus.
3. Everything from this station hits the ATEM, so Blue can grab stills.

*Open:* which lenses to lay out, and whether there's a prime/zoom pairing worth naming on the card.

### 3. Camera obscura

*What it's about (Marlon):* the chain. From the beginning, using a camera meant passing an image through stages on the way from reality to a work of art: projection, then drawing, then painting; later negative and print, with developing as its own step; or direct positives like tintype and cyanotype where the final image is made right there. Each stage adds something. The recursion loop is one more case of the same thing.

*The theory line, kept light (Marlon):* anti-hylomorphism. It isn't that you have a vision that gets perfectly realized in the work. There's something exploratory and labour-like about wrestling with the medium, the way a carver's tools meet the resistance of the grain and leave marks. Ruskin on the Gothic: those imperfections are interesting and beautiful. In the age of AI they may come to look like the mark of humanity. Ingold's *Making* is the companion; Pye's "workmanship of risk" vs. "workmanship of certainty" is the same distinction in craft terms and fits a card.

*Recipe (decided in outline):*
1. A couple of setups with the large-format lenses onto ground glass and frosted acrylic. Focus by moving the lens and the screen relative to each other. See the image inverted; see vignetting, hot spot, falloff.
2. Photograph the acrylic with a phone. The phone stands where the draughtsman stood. Notice what the phone adds: exposure for the hot spot, white balance, the frosting texture.
3. Recursion: point a camera into its own feed on the screen. Watch what each pass adds.
4. Madeleine's prints are at this station.

*Terms on the card (Claude's suggestion, from the existing glossary):* image plane, ground glass, inversion, vignetting, hot spot, depth of field as it relates to sensor size, workmanship of risk.

### 4. Phone

*What's here (decided):* the phone tripods students are being given, the DJI gimbal, front vs. rear cameras, recording modes, manual-control apps, and the wireless mic.

*Recipe (decided in outline):*
1. Set up the tripod they're keeping. Then the gimbal: a short walking shot, and the same walk handheld for comparison.
2. Front vs. rear camera. Shoot the same thing with each and compare.
3. Recording modes: resolution, frame rate, codec, bit depth (the "five numbers" docs page already covers this).
4. Manual control. The stock app applies ML-based correction they may not want; it can make dramatic lighting hard to keep because the phone flattens it. Try a manual app and lock exposure and white balance.
5. Wireless mic to the phone. Record with the built-in mic, then with the wireless mic clipped close, and compare. The one audio lesson of the day: distance to the mic beats everything else.

*Terms on the card:* lock (AE/AF/WB), gimbal, front vs. rear, the five numbers, computational photography.

## Intake and the live wall

*Decided:* two intake paths, both manual today (above). `/live` shows whatever lands in `_media/`.

*Marlon's proposals for later, not for today:* something semi-automated where Blue's captures (OBS stills, or Final Cut markers) land in a drop folder that gets processed; an AI pass that analyzes each shot and displays whatever it can say about it.

*Claude's suggestion:* since there is no metadata, the AI caption has to come from the pixels. A vision-model pass per image that names the lighting it sees, or guesses which station the shot came from, would give the wall captions and double as the day's only visible AI. A filename prefix by station (`01-`, `02-`…) is the cheap way to group if someone can add it on the way in; otherwise let the model guess the station.

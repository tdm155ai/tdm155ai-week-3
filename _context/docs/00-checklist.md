# Checklist: tools of the AV trade

Today is hands-on. Every station has a thing to do, a thing to notice, and a thing to share. Work in pairs or threes, rotate when a station is free, and don't try to finish all ten; four or five done well is the goal. Words you don't know are in the [glossary](../glossary/README.md). What you shoot goes on the [live wall](03-sharing-to-live.md) in the room.

Three ideas run through everything below:

- **Distance does more than settings.** Move the mic, the light, or the lens before you touch a dial.
- **Lock it.** A phone on auto keeps changing its mind mid-shot. Manual apps exist so you can stop it.
- **A phone is a camera with a tiny sensor and a fixed lens.** Once you have seen what a big lens does on a big piece of glass, every phone "portrait mode" makes sense as a simulation of it.

## 1. Sound: wireless mics and the phone

Gear: a wireless mic set (transmitter + receiver), your phone, headphones.

- [ ] Plug the receiver into your phone. Turn the transmitter on and wait for the link light.
- [ ] Open the camera app you'll use and confirm it is recording from the external mic, not the phone. Scratch the transmitter mic lightly while recording; you should hear it.
- [ ] Record the same two sentences three ways: phone's own mic at arm's length; phone's own mic across the table; wireless mic clipped 6–8 in below your chin.
- [ ] Play all three back on headphones.

Notice: how much room you hear in each; what happens when the speaker turns their head; whether the phone mic picks up your hand on the case. Note any latency between lips and sound on the wireless version.

Share: the three clips, named `mic-phone-near`, `mic-phone-far`, `mic-wireless`.

## 2. Sound: condenser vs. dynamic

Gear: one [condenser](../glossary/condenser-mic.md) mic and one [dynamic](../glossary/dynamic-mic.md) mic on stands, into the same recorder or interface, headphones.

- [ ] Speak into each at the same distance (about a hand's width). Match the levels so the meter peaks in the same place.
- [ ] Back off to arm's length and speak again on each.
- [ ] Get close, a couple of inches, and speak on each. Listen for the bass bump on the dynamic ([proximity effect](../glossary/proximity-effect.md)).
- [ ] Tap the table, rustle paper, and whisper, once per mic.
- [ ] Find the [polar pattern](../glossary/polar-pattern.md) on each: walk around the mic while talking.

Notice: the condenser hears more of everything, including the room and the HVAC; the dynamic needs you close and rejects more. Decide which you'd hand a nervous interviewee in a noisy hallway, and which you'd use for a voiceover in a quiet room.

Share: one short A/B clip that makes the difference obvious.

## 3. Movement: the DJI gimbal

Gear: a DJI Osmo Mobile [gimbal](../glossary/gimbal.md), your phone.

- [ ] Mount the phone, balance it, power on, and let it level.
- [ ] Shoot a 10-second walk toward a classmate three ways: handheld, handheld with the phone's own stabilization on, and on the gimbal.
- [ ] Try the modes: pan-follow (the default), tilt-lock, and FPV/free. Do a slow pan and a slow orbit around a still object.
- [ ] Try the trigger: hold to lock, double-tap to recenter.

Notice: the gimbal smooths rotation but not your footsteps; the bob is still there until you bend your knees and walk heel-to-toe. Zoom in on the screen after each pass and compare edge wobble.

Share: the three walks, side by side if you can.

## 4. Support: tripod and selfie stick

Gear: the magnetic [tripod](../glossary/tripod.md) / [selfie stick](../glossary/selfie-stick.md) combo, your phone.

- [ ] Set the phone at eye level on the tripod, an arm's length away, rear camera. Frame yourself with eyes a third of the way down.
- [ ] Now drop it to chest height and shoot the same frame. Then raise it above your head.
- [ ] Extend the stick and use it as a third arm: a high-angle shot of the table, a low shot along the floor, a shot around a corner.
- [ ] Do a two-person interview frame with the stick as the boom: phone on the stick, held by a third person just out of frame.

Notice: the lens-below-the-chin look; how much a foot of height changes a face; what the stick lets you reach that your arm can't.

Share: the eye-level, low, and high versions of the same portrait.

## 5. Light: three-point lighting with panels

Gear: two bi-color LED panels, one pocket RGB panel, a piece of foam core, a subject on a stool.

Build it in order, one source at a time, looking at the phone screen after each step. Overheads off.

- [ ] [Key](../glossary/key-light.md) only: panel at 10 or 2 o'clock, above eye level, tilted down, diffuser on. Look for a soft shadow on the far side of the nose and a catchlight in both eyes.
- [ ] Add [fill](../glossary/fill-light.md): foam core on the opposite side first; swap it for the second panel at low power if that isn't enough.
- [ ] Add the [back light](../glossary/back-light.md): the pocket panel behind and above, aimed at the back of the head, or at the wall for a color.
- [ ] Turn each one off in turn and look at what disappears.
- [ ] Move the key from 3 ft to 18 in and watch it get brighter *and* softer at once ([inverse-square law](../glossary/inverse-square-law.md)).
- [ ] Set one panel to 3200 K and the other to 5600 K. Look at the face. Then match them.

Notice: flat vs. shaped; the subject melting into the wall without the back light; the orange/blue split when [color temperatures](../glossary/color-temperature.md) mix.

Share: key only, key + fill, all three, as three stills of the same person.

## 6. Cameras: the big lens and the ground glass

Gear: a [large-format](../glossary/large-format.md) lens on a stand, a [ground glass](../glossary/ground-glass.md) or frosted acrylic sheet, a dark corner of the room, a subject with a light on them.

- [ ] Point the lens at your subject and hold the glass behind it. Slide the glass back and forth until the image snaps into [focus](../glossary/focus.md). That distance is the lens's focal length, more or less. The image is upside down; that is correct.
- [ ] Have your subject step toward the lens. Refocus by moving the glass. Notice which direction you moved.
- [ ] Focus on a near object and look at what happened to the far one. That band of sharpness is [depth of field](../glossary/depth-of-field.md).
- [ ] If the lens has an iris, close it down two stops and refocus. The band gets deeper and the image gets dimmer ([aperture](../glossary/aperture.md)).
- [ ] Put a string of small lights or a window in the background and throw it out of focus. Those discs are [bokeh](../glossary/bokeh.md).
- [ ] Now hold an iPhone up to the projected image circle. Compare the size of that circle to the phone's [sensor](../glossary/sensor-size.md), which is smaller than your fingernail.

Notice: a big image needs a big lens far from the glass, and that geometry is where shallow focus comes from. A phone's lens is millimeters from its sensor, so nearly everything is sharp, and "portrait mode" is software guessing which parts to blur. The whole rig is a [camera obscura](../glossary/camera-obscura.md) with a lens.

Share: a phone photo of the image on the glass (see station 9).

## 7. Manual control on the phone

Gear: your phone with [Blackmagic Camera](../glossary/manual-camera-app.md) installed (free, iPhone and Android). Another manual app is fine.

- [ ] Point at the lit subject from station 5. Watch auto exposure drift as someone walks through the frame. Then lock exposure, focus, and white balance.
- [ ] Turn on the [histogram](../glossary/histogram.md) and [zebras](../glossary/zebra.md) at 95%. Raise exposure until stripes appear on the forehead, then back off.
- [ ] Set [ISO](../glossary/iso.md) to the minimum and add light instead. Then push ISO to the max and look at the noise in the shadows.
- [ ] Set 24 fps and a 180° [shutter](../glossary/shutter-speed.md). Wave a hand. Change to 45° and wave again. Then 360°.
- [ ] Set [white balance](../glossary/white-balance.md) to 3200 K under the 5600 K panel, then the reverse.

Notice: how much the phone was deciding for you; what motion blur looks like at each shutter angle; that the histogram shows a clipped highlight before your eye does.

Share: the same frame at two shutter angles.

## 8. Recording formats

Gear: your phone's own camera settings (Settings → Camera on iPhone) and Blackmagic Camera's format menu. Full reference: [recording formats](01-recording-formats.md).

- [ ] Find your phone's current settings: [resolution](../glossary/resolution.md), [frame rate](../glossary/frame-rate.md), [codec](../glossary/codec.md) (HEVC / H.265 vs. H.264), HDR on or off ([bit depth](../glossary/bit-depth.md)), and whether ProRes or a [log profile](../glossary/log-profile.md) is available.
- [ ] Record 10 seconds at 1080p30 H.264 and 10 seconds at 4K60 HEVC (or the highest your phone offers). Compare file sizes.
- [ ] If the phone offers ProRes or Log: record 10 seconds and compare that file size to the others.
- [ ] Check what the *photo* format is: HEIC or JPEG. HEIC won't display in a browser or on the live wall; set Most Compatible for today, or convert when you share.

Notice: resolution is only one of five numbers that decide what a file looks like and how big it is. The [bitrate](../glossary/bitrate.md) is the one you never see in the menu.

Share: a screenshot of your format settings, and the file sizes in the caption.

## 9. Experiments: old techniques, new phones

Gear: everything above, plus a monitor or laptop showing a live camera feed.

- [ ] **Video feedback.** Point a camera at the screen showing its own feed. Tilt it a few degrees. Zoom in slowly. ([video feedback](../glossary/video-feedback.md), a [mise en abyme](../glossary/mise-en-abyme.md).)
- [ ] **Photograph the projection.** Photograph the upside-down image of a classmate on the ground glass with your phone. Turn the phone over so the picture reads right way up. Try it on the frosted acrylic; the texture is different.
- [ ] **Shoot through the lens.** Put the phone's lens where the glass was and shoot through the big lens directly. Vignetting and softness are the point.
- [ ] **Phone of a phone.** Photograph another phone's screen showing its camera view of you. Then swap.
- [ ] **Recurse the wall.** Photograph the live wall while your last photo is on it, and share that.

Notice: which of these look like "mistakes" and which look like something you'd keep. Write the one you'd keep in your caption.

Share: at least one.

## 10. Before you leave

- [ ] Everything you want on the wall is in `_media/` (see [sharing to /live](03-sharing-to-live.md)), as JPEG, PNG, MP4, or MOV.
- [ ] Footage you care about is off the phone and backed up.
- [ ] Mics back in their charging cases. Panels off, diffusers on, stands folded. Gimbal powered down and folded.
- [ ] Lens capped; ground glass and acrylic back in their sleeves.
- [ ] One line in the channel: the station that surprised you, and why.

Weekly turn-in: a video, either two short tutorials or two contrasting shots or scenes, using something from today. The [desk video kit](02-desk-video-kit.md) page is the setup you can take home.

# Station 1: Studio lights

Gear: two bi-color LED panels (one small, one large), a pocket RGB panel, foam core, a stool, the studio camera feeding OBS on the connected computer, and the capture button.

Positions use a clock face seen from above: the subject sits at the center facing the camera at 12.

## Recipe

1. **The base setup.** Someone sits. Overheads off. Key light at **2:00 or 10:00**, above eye level, tilted down, diffuser on. Back light **opposite the key** (if the key is at 2, the back light is at 8), behind and above, on the hair and shoulders. Look for a catchlight in both eyes and a soft shadow on the far side of the nose.
2. **With fill, without fill.** Capture one still with the fill in (foam core on the side opposite the key, or the second panel at low power). Pull the fill and capture the same frame again. Two stills: fill / no fill.
3. **Around the clock.** Take the fill and the back light out; leave only the key. Now move the key around the clock and capture a still at every hour: 12, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (straight behind), 7, 8, 9, 10, 11. Do the whole circle twice: once with the subject **head on** to the camera, once in **profile**. Keep the key at the same height and distance each time so the only thing changing is the angle. That's up to 24 stills; press the button each time.
4. **Hard vs. soft, if there's time.** Small panel far back with no diffuser; large panel close with the diffuser. Same face, two stills. What changed is the apparent size of the source, not its brightness.

## Notice

Watch the clock series play back on the wall. 12 is flat; 2 and 10 are the portrait angles; 3 and 9 split the face; 6 is a silhouette with a rim. The catchlight moves around the eye as the key moves around the clock. That's the vocabulary: when you like a creator's lighting, you can now say where the key was.

## Share

The capture button sends each still to the wall. Name nothing; just press it. Fill / no fill, then the two clock series.

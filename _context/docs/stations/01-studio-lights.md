# Station 1: Studio lights

Gear at this station: **large and small bi-color LED panels**, **reflectors** (white and silver, plus foam core), **adjustable RGB lights** that can be any color, a **ring light**, a stool, the studio camera feeding OBS on the connected computer, and the capture button.

One rule covers most of it: **the bigger the light looks from where the subject sits, the softer it is.** A large panel is softer than a small one; any panel gets softer as you bring it closer. Small and far away is hard light with crisp shadow edges. There is no diffuser at this station; size and distance are the two dials.

Positions use a clock face seen from above: the subject sits at the center facing the camera at 12.

## Recipe

1. **The base setup.** Someone sits. Overheads off. Key light at **2:00 or 10:00**, above eye level, tilted down; use the large panel. Back light **opposite the key** (if the key is at 2, the back light is at 8), behind and above, on the hair and shoulders. Look for a catchlight in both eyes and a soft shadow on the far side of the nose.
2. **With fill, without fill.** Capture one still with the fill in (foam core on the side opposite the key, or the second panel at low power). Pull the fill and capture the same frame again. Two stills: fill / no fill.
3. **Around the clock.** Take the fill and the back light out; leave only the key. Now move the key around the clock and capture a still at every hour: 12, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (straight behind), 7, 8, 9, 10, 11. Do the whole circle twice: once with the subject **head on** to the camera, once in **profile**. Keep the key at the same height and distance each time so the only thing changing is the angle. That's up to 24 stills; press the button each time.

## Extra challenges

- **Hard vs. soft.** Same face, two stills: the small panel far back, then the large panel brought in close. Look at the shadow edge and the nose-to-cheek transition. What changed is the apparent size of the source, not its brightness.
- **Fill with a reflector.** Rebuild the base setup, but make the fill with a reflector instead of a panel: hold the white or silver side just out of frame on the side opposite the key and angle it at the face until the shadow side lifts. Capture with and without.
- **Color contrast.** Swap the RGB lights in for two of the three points and set them to different colors: a warm key and a cool back light, or a key in one color and the fill in its opposite (orange and teal, magenta and green). Capture it. Then try one color on the wall behind the subject and white on the face.
- **The ring light.** Put it on the camera axis, right around the lens, and capture. Look at the eyes: one round catchlight, centered, and almost no shadows on the face. That's the look of a lot of what you watch; now you know what made it.

## Notice

Watch the clock series play back on the wall. 12 is flat; 2 and 10 are the portrait angles; 3 and 9 split the face; 6 is a silhouette with a rim. The catchlight moves around the eye as the key moves around the clock. That's the vocabulary: when you like a creator's lighting, you can now say where the key was.

## Share

The capture button sends each still to the wall. Name nothing; just press it. Fill / no fill, then the two clock series.
